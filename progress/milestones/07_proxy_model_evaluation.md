## Main comparative findings

The proxy evaluation confirms that perturbations do not have uniform effects across models.

For the primary generation target `efficientnet_b0`, the strongest degradation is produced by `sigma_zero`, followed by `fgsm` and `superdeepfool`.

| Evaluated model | Perturbation | Clean accuracy | Perturbed accuracy | Accuracy drop |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `efficientnet_b0` | `sigma_zero` | 0.978 | 0.015 | 0.963 |
| `efficientnet_b0` | `fgsm` | 0.978 | 0.575 | 0.403 |
| `efficientnet_b0` | `superdeepfool` | 0.978 | 0.931 | 0.047 |
| `efficientnet_b0` | `one_pixel` | 0.978 | 0.976 | 0.002 |
| `efficientnet_b0` | `color_shift` | 0.978 | 0.977 | 0.001 |

Anti-forensic transformations produce more limited but operationally relevant drops, especially for Gaussian blur and histogram modification depending on the evaluated model.

## Methodological notes

Binary samples are evaluated with the checkpoint corresponding to their fold. This preserves the fold-aware protocol and prevents each image from being scored by a model trained on the same fold.

OOD samples are evaluated separately and are not included in binary accuracy. This preserves the distinction between nominal binary classification and out-of-distribution behavior.

Comparative metrics match perturbed predictions to clean predictions through model, fold, and original image identifier. This allows attack-specific and model-specific analysis of induced errors.

This milestone is separate from the later commercial forensic-tool evaluation. Proxy models provide transparent, reproducible local baselines; commercial tools remain black-box systems evaluated in Milestone 09.
