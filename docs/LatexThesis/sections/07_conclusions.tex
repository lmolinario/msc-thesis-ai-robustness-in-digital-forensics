\section{Summary of the Research}
\label{sec:conclusions-summary}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This thesis investigated the operational robustness of \gls{ai}-based image
classification and semantic-retrieval systems used in \gls{df}. Its starting
point was that performance on clean, well-structured data does not fully
describe how automated triage behaves when evidence is heterogeneous,
degraded, outside the nominal task, or deliberately manipulated. In practice,
the direction of an error matters: a missed \texttt{weapon} image may not
receive appropriate priority, whereas an incorrect positive assignment may
increase the analyst's review workload.

The study therefore treated clean, adversarial, anti-forensic, and \gls{ood}
inputs as separate evaluation conditions. It combined three transparent proxy
models---\gls{efficientnetb0}, \gls{resnet18}, and a \gls{clip}-based
proxy---with six frozen configurations of four selected black-box software
systems: \gls{magnetaxiomtool}/\gls{magnetai},
\gls{griffeye}/\gls{t3kcore}, \gls{excirefoto2025}, and
\gls{cellebriteinseyets}. The proxy models provided inspectable predictions,
maximum predicted-class probabilities, matched error transitions, and
gradients. The black-box systems were assessed only through outputs observable
to the analyst and explicitly documented normalization rules; no equivalence
with the proxy architectures was assumed.

Both evaluation levels used the same frozen source population of 1\,500
images: 500 \texttt{weapon}, 500 \texttt{non\_weapon}, and 500 reserved for a
separate \gls{ood} branch. The 1\,000-image binary subset was used to generate
5\,000 adversarial and 5\,000 anti-forensic artifacts. Together with the clean
binary and \gls{ood} inputs, these formed an 11\,500-item forensic evaluation
bundle.
The perturbed files remained derived versions of the same 1\,000 binary source
images and were not treated as independent cases.

The complete procedure was formalized as the \gls{fairlab} protocol. FAIRLab
is neither a classifier nor a standalone software platform; it is a
task-specific methodological procedure linking human-validated dataset
construction, deterministic partitioning, proxy-model analysis, controlled
perturbation, blind-bundle preparation, black-box normalization, quantitative
and qualitative analysis, and traceability controls.

The central conclusion is that robustness is not an intrinsic property that
can be inferred from clean accuracy. It is conditional on the task, input
population, threat model, system version, configuration, observable output, and
normalization rule. Automated image-analysis outputs can assist forensic
triage, but they remain most defensible when they are traceable, reviewable,
and subordinate to expert assessment
\cite{costantini2019digital,sanna2024preliminary,sanna2025pixelperfect}.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
\label{sec:conclusions-research-questions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

\paragraph{\textbf{RQ1. To what extent do the transparent proxy models maintain
their clean-baseline performance under the separate \gls{ood}, adversarial,
and anti-forensic conditions?}}

Only to a condition-dependent extent. Clean accuracies are 0.978 for
\gls{efficientnetb0}, 0.964 for \gls{resnet18}, and 0.927 for the
\gls{clip}-based proxy, but these values do not remain uniformly representative
outside the clean branch.

The five anti-forensic transformations generally cause smaller aggregate
variations than the strongest adversarial attacks. The largest anti-forensic
accuracy reduction is 0.030 for \gls{resnet18} under
\gls{histogrammodification}. Aggregate stability is not equivalent to
sample-level invariance, however, because new errors may be offset by previous
errors that become correct. By contrast, direct-target attacks cause the most
substantial degradation: \gls{efficientnetb0} accuracy decreases to 0.575
under \gls{fgsm} and to 0.015 under \gls{sigmazero}.

The separate \gls{ood} branch reveals a further behavior that clean binary
accuracy cannot predict. Fold-aggregated weapon assignment rates are 0.3696,
0.4376, and 0.8356 for \gls{efficientnetb0}, \gls{resnet18}, and the
\gls{clip}-based proxy, respectively. These are forced closed-set assignments,
not supervised correctness measures. Clean performance, anti-forensic
sensitivity, direct adversarial vulnerability, and \gls{ood} behavior must
therefore be evaluated as distinct dimensions
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-clean-baseline}--\ref{sec:results-adversarial}).

\paragraph{\textbf{RQ2. How do direct source-model attacks,
cross-architecture transfer, and the model-agnostic \gls{colorshift} condition
differ in their effects on the evaluated proxy models?}}

Direct source-model attacks have substantially larger effects than the observed
cross-architecture transfer. The four model-dependent attacks were generated
against fold-specific \gls{efficientnetb0} checkpoints, so their evaluation on
that architecture is direct-target, whereas results for \gls{resnet18} and the
\gls{clip}-based proxy measure empirical transfer.

The clearest direct-target failure is \gls{sigmazero}: accuracy falls to 0.015
and 963 of 978 clean-correct samples become incorrect. \gls{fgsm} reduces
accuracy to 0.575 and introduces 406 errors, mainly in the
\texttt{weapon}-to-\texttt{non\_weapon} direction. On the non-target models,
the largest transferred degradation occurs under \gls{fgsm}, with accuracy
decreasing from 0.964 to 0.918 for \gls{resnet18} and from 0.927 to 0.914 for
the \gls{clip}-based proxy. Transferred \gls{sigmazero} artifacts do not
reproduce the direct-target collapse.

Transferability must therefore be measured rather than assumed. Its limited
effect here does not demonstrate direct robustness of the non-target models,
because no matched attacks were optimized against them. \gls{colorshift} is
also analytically separate: it is deterministic and model-agnostic and is
interpreted as a condition-specific transformation rather than direct or
transferred attack success (Section~\ref{sec:results-adversarial}).

\paragraph{\textbf{RQ3. How do the selected black-box software systems respond
to the same frozen experimental corpus, and how do observable output semantics
and configuration choices affect the resulting operational profiles?}}

The systems produce heterogeneous operating profiles despite processing the
same frozen bundle. Their decisions originate from tags, bookmarks, exported
labels, or semantic-retrieval membership; the metrics are consequently
conditional on the normalization adopted for each configuration.

Under the frozen protocol, \gls{griffeye}/\gls{t3kcore} has a selective
profile, with bundle-weighted accuracy of 0.972 and \gls{fpr} of 0.007, under
the conservative use of only \texttt{CORE/Violence/Firearm} as the primary
positive bookmark. \gls{cellebriteinseyets} is comparatively balanced, with
bundle accuracy, weapon recall, and weapon precision close to 0.958.
\gls{magnetaxiomtool}/\gls{magnetai} has bundle accuracy of 0.933 and
\gls{fnr} of 0.099, with greater missed-target exposure under some conditions.

Configuration sensitivity is clearest for \gls{excirefoto2025}. On clean
inputs, moving from \texttt{D20} to \texttt{D80} reduces the \gls{fnr} from
0.120 to 0.014 but increases the \gls{fpr} from 0.026 to 0.156 and the
\gls{ood} weapon assignment rate from 0.238 to 0.522. Under transferred
\gls{sigmazero}, the \gls{fnr} falls from 0.180 to 0.024 while the \gls{fpr}
rises from 0.190 to 0.662.

No universal ranking follows from these results. Restrictive configurations
may reduce review workload while increasing missed detections; permissive
configurations may do the reverse. Software version, enabled functions,
category or query settings, observable semantics, and normalization are all
part of the reported operating profile
(Section~\ref{sec:results-commercial}).

\paragraph{\textbf{RQ4. Which error transitions, robustness degradations,
\gls{ood} assignment behaviors, and unknown output states are most relevant
to forensic triage and continued human review?}}

The most consequential transition for triage is
\texttt{weapon}\(\rightarrow\)\texttt{non\_weapon}, because it may prevent
relevant content from receiving priority. The reverse transition remains
important because it increases review workload. Aggregate change must therefore
be interpreted together with error direction: the direct \gls{sigmazero}
result combines near-total collapse with both types of error, while transferred
conditions produce different false-negative and false-positive burdens across
the commercial configurations.

The \gls{ood} branch creates a separate operational concern. A
\texttt{weapon} assignment on an \gls{ood} input is not a supervised false
positive, but it can still alter prioritization; every evaluated proxy and
black-box configuration produces a non-zero rate. Maximum predicted-class
probability must also remain separate from reliability: the representative
\gls{sigmazero} failure reaches 1.000 despite being incorrect.

No \texttt{unknown} output occurs in the canonical 69\,000 normalized
black-box decisions, so it is not an observed failure mode in this experiment.
Explicit unknown handling nevertheless remains necessary for future versions,
alternative exports, and incomplete mappings. The operational response is to
report missed-target risk, false-positive workload, and \gls{ood} assignments
separately while preserving system versions, configurations, output mappings,
source-to-decision traceability, and continued human review
(Sections~\ref{sec:results-comparative} and
\ref{sec:results-limitations}).

\section{Main Contributions}
\label{sec:conclusions-contributions}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The thesis makes four connected contributions.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{A forensic robustness protocol.}
It formalizes \gls{fairlab} as a methodological procedure for evaluating
\gls{ai}-based image analysis beyond clean accuracy. The protocol coordinates
human-validated data construction, deterministic partitioning, transparent
proxy analysis, perturbation generation, a separate \gls{ood} branch,
black-box evaluation, output normalization, explainability, and traceability.
Its contribution is the integration of these components into one reproducible
and auditable process, not the proposal of a new classifier or defensive
mechanism.

\item \textbf{A common frozen experimental basis.}
The same 1\,500-image source population supports both evaluation levels. Five
adversarial or adversarial-style conditions and five anti-forensic conditions
are generated from the 1\,000-image binary subset and combined with the clean
binary and \gls{ood} branches in the 11\,500-item bundle. Stable source links
ensure that the 10\,000 perturbed artifacts remain identifiable as derived
source--condition combinations rather than independent cases.

\item \textbf{A parallel transparent and black-box evaluation.}
The proxy models provide inspectable checkpoints, predictions, matched error
transitions, maximum predicted-class probabilities, and gradients. The
commercial systems expose heterogeneous tags, bookmarks, classification
labels, and semantic-query results. Explicit mappings normalize these outputs
for operational comparison without implying common internal architectures or
taxonomies. The design also distinguishes attacks evaluated directly against
\gls{efficientnetb0} from empirical transfer to non-target models and
black-box configurations.

\item \textbf{An auditable, error-oriented research artifact.}
Identifiers, manifests, cryptographic digests, checkpoints, normalized
predictions, metrics, and reporting assets preserve the path from each source
image to its experimental outcomes. The analysis emphasizes missed-target risk,
false-positive review workload, \gls{ood} assignment behavior, configuration
sensitivity, and residual metadata limitations rather than reducing robustness
to one score. Five purposively selected \gls{ig} cases add qualitative
proxy-model diagnostics without being treated as quantitative evidence or as
explanations of the black-box systems.

\end{enumerate}

Taken together, these contributions define and apply a traceable procedure for
revealing how heterogeneous automated systems behave when the assumptions of
clean, closed-set evaluation no longer hold.

performance and operational robustness are related but non-equivalent
properties. High clean accuracy provides an important baseline, but it does not
predict how a system will behave under distributional mismatch, image-level
transformations, adversarial manipulation, or alternative operational
configurations.

The clean results already expose different error tradeoffs. Although
\gls{efficientnetb0} makes the fewest clean errors among the proxy models, the
\gls{clip}-based proxy combines the lowest clean \gls{fnr} (0.012) with the
highest clean \gls{fpr} (0.134). Reducing missed targets can therefore increase
the quantity of material presented for review.

The separate \gls{ood} analysis adds a dimension that clean binary results do
not capture. Both proxy models and black-box configurations assign some
out-of-task inputs to the firearm-oriented state, but these assignments are not
ordinary false positives. Assignment frequency and maximum predicted-class
probability also describe different phenomena: the \gls{clip}-based proxy has
the highest \gls{ood} weapon assignment rate without any fold-specific response
above 0.90, whereas \gls{resnet18} produces fewer weapon assignments but the
highest high-maximum-probability rate.

Anti-forensic transformations generally have smaller aggregate effects than the
strongest direct-target attacks, yet their error direction remains
operationally relevant. Gaussian Blur, for example, introduces 17 new
\gls{efficientnetb0} errors, all in the
\texttt{weapon}-to-\texttt{non\_weapon} direction. A limited or negative
accuracy drop is therefore not evidence of unchanged behavior, because new
errors may be offset by previous errors that become correct.

The adversarial results provide the clearest direct failure: \gls{sigmazero}
changes 963 of the 978 clean-correct \gls{efficientnetb0} inputs into errors,
while \gls{fgsm} concentrates more strongly on missed targets. The same frozen
artifacts have smaller and heterogeneous effects on non-target models and
commercial configurations. Those observations quantify transfer only and
cannot be generalized as direct resistance to attacks optimized for each
system.

At the black-box level, the results remain inseparable from observable output
semantics and frozen configuration. Excire's \texttt{D20}--\texttt{D80}
settings demonstrate the clearest recall--selectivity tradeoff; Griffeye's low
\gls{fpr} depends on the conservative primary bookmark; and the Cellebrite and
Magnet profiles depend on their selected labels and tag. These are operating
profiles, not version-independent properties of the software families.

Finally, excluding the 15 files with predefined sensitive metadata-term
matches does not materially change aggregate results, but it cannot establish
that embedded metadata was unused. The five \gls{xai} cases likewise support
case-level inspection only. Together, the findings show that operational
robustness requires joint consideration of error direction, \gls{ood}
behavior, perturbation sensitivity, configuration, output mapping,
traceability, and human review.

\section{Operational and Forensic Implications}
\label{sec:conclusions-operational-implications}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The evidence supports automated image analysis as a traceable triage aid, not
as an autonomous mechanism for evidentiary decision-making. Its value lies in
helping analysts organize and prioritize large collections while leaving each
output open to reconstruction, source-image verification, and contextual
interpretation. Four operational principles follow from the results.

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{Evaluate consequences, not accuracy alone.}
Systems with similar aggregate accuracy may distribute their errors very
differently. A false negative may prevent firearm-oriented material from
receiving priority; a false positive increases review workload and may dilute
analyst attention. The appropriate balance is task-dependent: a context in
which missed targets are especially consequential may favor a permissive
configuration, whereas very large evidence volumes may require greater
selectivity. Condition-level metrics and error transitions must therefore
remain visible alongside aggregate performance.

\item \textbf{Keep \gls{ood} assignments and scores in their proper semantic
scope.}
An \gls{ood} image assigned to \texttt{weapon} has no supervised binary
reference assignment and is not an ordinary false positive, but it can still
trigger a flag or increase review workload. Anomalous, ambiguous, degraded,
synthetic, and semantically adjacent content should consequently be reported
separately and, where supported, exposed through rejection or unknown states.
Likewise, proxy-model maximum predicted-class probabilities are
architecture-specific diagnostic values, not cross-model measures of
correctness. Tags, bookmarks, exported percentages, labels, and retrieval
membership from commercial systems have different semantics and must not be
collapsed into a common probability scale without independent validation.

\item \textbf{Treat configuration and version as part of the system.}
Excire's \texttt{Distance limit}, Griffeye's positive bookmark, Cellebrite's
selected export labels, and Magnet.AI's \texttt{Possible weapons} tag directly
shape the observed profiles. A result is therefore inseparable from the
software or model version, enabled functions, checkpoint or configuration,
category or query, output format, and normalization rule. Updates may alter
preprocessing, weights, thresholds, taxonomies, retrieval behavior, or export
structures; validation from one frozen version must not be transferred to a
later version without verification.

\item \textbf{Preserve source-to-decision traceability.}
Each output should remain linked to the source item and its integrity record,
the processing condition, the observable system output, the applied mapping,
and the subsequent analyst review. This chain helps distinguish genuine system
behavior from incomplete exports, missing records, data-processing errors, or
incorrect normalization. A result whose provenance cannot be reconstructed has
limited analytical value in a forensic workflow.

\end{enumerate}

The metadata analysis illustrates the need to define traceability and blindness
precisely. Opaque names and directories conceal experimental assignments at the
file-system level but do not remove information embedded in the image bytes.
The leave-out analysis supports the stability of the aggregate conclusions;
it does not prove that metadata was never accessed. Claims of blind processing
should therefore identify whether they concern paths, embedded metadata,
pixels, or all available channels.

The qualitative \gls{xai} layer provides limited diagnostic transparency for
selected \gls{efficientnetb0} cases by linking input, prediction, maximum
predicted-class probability, attribution target, and visualization. The maps do
not identify causal features, certify correctness, or explain the black-box
systems. Explainability remains supplementary to quantitative robustness tests
and expert assessment.

These principles define a \textit{human-in-the-loop} use model. The analyst
retains responsibility for interpreting flagged and, where appropriate,
unflagged material, corroborating automated outputs with the remaining
evidence, and determining their final analytical significance. The findings do
not argue against \gls{ai}-assisted forensic triage; they specify the conditions
that make it more defensible: task-specific validation, an explicit threat
model, controlled configurations, version management, traceability, and
continued visibility of limitations.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusions-limitations}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

The findings apply within the scope of the frozen protocol and the defined case
study. They must not be interpreted as universal validation of the evaluated
models or software systems. The principal limitations are summarized below.

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{Dataset and semantic validation.}
The 1\,500 manually selected source images support a controlled case study, not
the full diversity or prevalence of operational evidence. The binary subset is
artificially balanced, and the \texttt{non\_weapon} branch is predominantly
derived from the \texttt{05\_deepweb} source group. Moreover, the documented
\textit{human-in-the-loop} assignments were produced by one reviewer without
independent annotation or an inter-rater agreement measure. The labels are
traceable but remain dependent on the adopted criteria and reviewer judgment.

\item \textbf{Definition and measurement of \gls{ood} behavior.}
The \gls{ood} branch intentionally combines borderline, degraded, synthetic,
non-firearm weapon, and semantically external content. It is neither a
homogeneous supervised class nor a universal representation of distributional
shift. Proxy rates aggregate 2\,500 fold-specific responses---five checkpoints
for each of 500 images---whereas each black-box configuration produces one
decision per image. The reported proportions are parallel operating tendencies,
not identical prediction units.

\item \textbf{Derived-sample dependence and finite conditions.}
The 5\,000 adversarial and 5\,000 anti-forensic files derive from the same
1\,000 clean binary images. Bundle-level results therefore concern
source--condition combinations rather than an independent 11\,000-case binary
population. Each perturbation also uses one frozen parameterization; different
budgets, severities, encodings, implementations, or transformation chains may
produce different profiles.

\item \textbf{Direct-target scope and artifact representation.}
The four model-dependent attacks were optimized only against fold-specific
\gls{efficientnetb0} checkpoints. Results for \gls{resnet18}, the
\gls{clip}-based proxy, and the black-box systems measure transfer and do not
establish direct robustness. In addition, \gls{fgsm}, \gls{sigmazero}, and
\gls{superdeepfool} were exported as \(224 \times 224\) \texttt{PNG} files,
while clean sources retained heterogeneous formats and dimensions;
\gls{onepixel} also used \texttt{PNG} serialization. Black-box responses may
therefore combine perturbation, resizing, and serialization effects.

\item \textbf{Proxy and black-box comparability.}
The proxy models are transparent experimental references, not validated
surrogates for proprietary internal models. Their gradients, probabilities,
and attributions cannot explain black-box behavior. Conversely, black-box
tags, bookmarks, labels, percentages, and retrieval membership have
heterogeneous semantics. Their operational recoding does not establish a
shared internal binary task, and alternative defensible mappings could produce
different profiles. All commercial findings remain specific to the frozen
software versions, enabled functions, configurations, exports, and
normalization rules.

\item \textbf{Qualitative explainability and residual metadata.}
The \gls{xai} analysis contains five purposively selected
\gls{efficientnetb0} cases and is not statistically representative. The
historical \gls{ig} assets use 32 integration steps and a zero baseline without
established numerical convergence, so they remain qualitative diagnostics. The
blind bundle also retains embedded image metadata: 15 files contain predefined
sensitive-term matches. Their exclusion has little aggregate effect but does
not determine whether a system accessed the embedded fields.

\item \textbf{Controlled-data and public-artifact boundary.}
Legal, ethical, institutional, licensing, and proprietary restrictions prevent
complete public redistribution of all source images, generated trees,
commercial exports, and prediction-level evidence. The repository preserves
scripts, schemas, manifests, configurations, digests, normalized summaries,
metrics, selected assets, and validation reports. This supports procedural
reproducibility and auditability, but full re-execution requires authorized
access to controlled data and proprietary outputs.

\item \textbf{Legal and evidentiary scope.}
The study evaluates technical and operational triage behavior. It does not
determine admissibility, probative value, or the evidentiary weight of an
automated classification. Those questions require the applicable legal
framework, chain-of-custody analysis, independent validation, disclosure
obligations, and case-specific interpretation.

\end{itemize}

These limitations delimit rather than invalidate the experiment. The findings
describe the observable behavior of the frozen systems, configurations,
mappings, population, and threat model. They do not certify a product,
establish a universal ranking, validate a production firearm detector, or
support autonomous evidentiary deployment.

\section{Future Work}
\label{sec:conclusions-future-work}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Future research directions derive directly from the experimental findings and
from the limitations of the frozen protocol. Six directions are especially
useful.

\paragraph{Broader data and stronger semantic validation.}
Future studies should use larger multi-source populations, realistic class
prevalence, additional firearm-related subcategories, and more varied negative
samples from different devices, platforms, acquisition procedures, and
media-processing pipelines. Multiple independent reviewers, formal agreement
measures, and an adjudication procedure would reduce reliance on a single
judgment and quantify ambiguity. The \gls{ood} population should also be
stratified into categories such as borderline firearm content, non-firearm
weapons, synthetic imagery, degraded media, video-game material, military
equipment, and semantically unrelated inputs. This would show whether closed-set
assignments concentrate in particular visual or semantic groups.

\paragraph{Explicit \gls{ood} handling and analyst-centered evaluation.}
Calibrated model-specific estimates, abstention thresholds, dedicated
\gls{ood} detectors, open-set recognition, and selective classification should
be compared as mechanisms for exposing unknown or ambiguous inputs. Their value
should be measured not only through classification metrics but also through
missed detections, false-positive workload, deferred cases, review time, and
final decision quality. Controlled user studies could compare workflows with
and without automated triage, measure analyst overrides, and test whether
uncertainty indicators, explicit unknown states, or explanation aids improve
the quality of human review.

\paragraph{Matched adversarial evaluation and representation controls.}
Direct-target attacks should be generated independently against
\gls{resnet18} and the \gls{clip}-based proxy, using harmonized access
assumptions and perturbation budgets where technically meaningful. This would
permit a clearer comparison of direct vulnerability and transferability.
Black-box transfer studies should also include clean controls matched to each
artifact's resolution and serialization format, so that perturbation effects
can be separated from resizing and encoding. Broader parameter sweeps,
alternative objectives, targeted attacks, patches, and physical or hybrid
delivery conditions would test whether the observed effects persist under
different threat models.

\paragraph{Anti-forensic chains and operationally evaluated defenses.}
The anti-forensic branch should cover multiple severities and sequential
transformations, including repeated JPEG encoding, platform-specific
recompression, varied resize ratios, cropping, partial occlusion, screenshots,
color-space conversion, and combined tonal and geometric changes. Defensive
strategies---such as adversarial training, robust preprocessing, input
randomization, ensembles, and anomaly detection---should be evaluated against
the same operational criteria. Recovery of aggregate accuracy is insufficient
if it increases missed targets, \gls{ood} assignments, computational cost, or
analyst workload.

\paragraph{Longitudinal black-box validation and metadata controls.}
Commercial systems should be re-evaluated across software versions, processing
modules, categories, and documented configurations. Longitudinal studies would
distinguish persistent behavior from release-specific effects and support
change-management rules for updates that modify preprocessing, thresholds,
taxonomies, retrieval logic, or export formats. Alternative defensible output
mappings should be compared, including broader bookmark or label combinations
and different Excire semantic queries and distance limits. A paired experiment
using metadata-preserving and metadata-neutralized copies of the same visual
content should separately measure the effect of embedded non-pixel information.

\paragraph{Explainability, replication, and extension of FAIRLab.}
The five historical \gls{ig} cases should be regenerated with the adaptive
convergence-validation procedure described in
Section~\ref{subsec:implementation-explainability}, explicit convergence
criteria, multiple baselines, and a larger systematic sample. Comparing
attribution methods and their stability across checkpoints and perturbations
could improve diagnostic reliability, while remaining supplementary to
quantitative testing rather than evidence of correctness or causality.

The protocol could then be applied to other forensic visual tasks, including
documents, screenshots, illicit substances, explicit or extremist content,
vehicles, faces, and logos. Each application would require a task-specific
definition, semantic criteria, threat model, output mapping, and operational
interpretation. Independent replication across laboratories, datasets,
software versions, and tasks is ultimately necessary to assess
generalizability.

Where complete redistribution is restricted, replication should still be
supported through schemas, scripts, configuration and version records,
manifests, stable identifiers, cryptographic digests, sanitized outputs,
condition-level metrics, and validation reports. This preserves the distinction
between procedural reproducibility and unrestricted access to controlled or
proprietary material.

\section{Closing Remarks}
\label{sec:conclusions-closing}
% ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

This thesis shows that clean accuracy is a necessary baseline but an
insufficient account of operational robustness in forensic image analysis.
Systems that perform well under nominal conditions may still miss relevant
content, increase false-positive workload, assign \gls{ood} inputs to a closed
operational category, respond differently after manipulation, or change
materially with configuration.

The parallel evaluation of transparent proxy models and selected black-box
configurations on the same frozen source population makes these differences
observable without conflating inspectable model behavior with proprietary
outputs. The \gls{fairlab} protocol provides the traceable structure needed to
connect data selection, perturbations, configurations, outputs, normalization,
metrics, and reviewable analytical records.

No evaluated system minimizes every operational risk. High recall may increase
review workload; greater selectivity may increase missed detections; strong
clean performance may coexist with direct adversarial failure. These tradeoffs
rule out a universal ranking and make validation conditional on the task,
threat model, version, configuration, and output mapping.

Automated classifications, tags, bookmarks, and semantic retrieval results
should therefore be used as triage indicators rather than autonomous
evidentiary conclusions. Their usefulness depends on source-to-output
traceability, separate visibility of error directions and \gls{ood} behavior,
and corroboration within the wider investigative context.

Operational robustness must be demonstrated under the conditions in which a
system is expected to operate; it cannot be presumed from nominal performance,
proprietary status, or an apparently confident output. In forensic image
analysis, automation is most defensible as a controlled, auditable, and
reviewable component of a \textit{human-in-the-loop} workflow.
